## Review Questions

**A.1 Explain what an end device is and how it is important for network communications.**

An end device is a device on the edge of a network that is either the source or the destination of data transmitted across the network.

**A.2 Explain what an intermediary device's functions are.**

Intermediary devices connect end devices to the network. Examples would be a switch or a router.

**A.3 What does network media do?**

Network media transfers information across the network. Examples would be LAN cables, Fiber-optic cables, or Wi-Fi.

**A.4 What are three types of network media you will find?**

LAN cables, fiber-optic cables, and wireless signals.

**A.5 What is the difference between a physical topology and a logical topology?**

Physical topology shows the physical location of network devices on a diagram. Logical topology shows how the devices on the network are connected.

**A.6 What are the differences between the four different size networks you will encounter?**

- Small home networks connect a few devices to each other and the internet.
- Small office or home office networks connect devices to each other and a centralized network off-site such as a corporate network.
- Medium to large networks will connect many locations with hundreds or thousands of connected hosts.
- World-wide networks connect the entire world, the internet is a great example of this.

**A.7 What is the difference between a LAN and a WAN?**

A LAN is a local area network. It connects a few devices on a smaller network locally. Some examples would be a home network or a school network.

A WAN is a wide area network. It connects several local area networks to one another using multiple providers. A large corporation with many locations would use a WAN to connect the locations to one-another.

**A.8 Explain fault tolerance and why it is so important in a network.**

Fault tolerance is the ability to stay connected if something within the network fails. It is important because it will keep critical information available during an outage.

**A.9 Explain the three primary requirements of network security.**

- Confidentiality-data stays between the people intended to have it.
- Integrity-making sure the data has not been corrupted
- Availability-making sure you can access the data whenever it is needed.

**A.10 Explain the concept of BYOD and how it will affect your career as an IT professional.**

BYOD means bring your own device. It is the concept that any person can use any device that they want to.

It affects my career because I will need to develop apps that work on many different ecosystems.

**A.11 Explain the common external threats to our networks.**

- Viruses - malicious software or code that takes over a computer or network
- Spyware - collects information about the user without permission
- Zero-Day - threats that happen on the first known day
- Threat actor - a malicious person attacking a network or device
- Denial of service - slowing or crashing apps on a network
- Data interception or theft - stealing information as it is being transmitted
- Identity theft - stealing personal information of a user
